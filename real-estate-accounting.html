Real estate accounting

Real estate accounting for commercial tenants and property owners

CAM and lease compliance review is the main service I provide. I compare the annual reconciliation your landlord issues against the terms of your lease, and I document the charges the lease does not support.

Who this is for

Commercial tenants in office, medical office, retail, and multi-tenant industrial space. Small real estate owners and investors who need the accounting side of a property handled.

CAM and lease compliance review

I review the annual CAM and operating expense reconciliation against your lease terms and identify charges the lease does not support. That includes management fees calculated on the wrong base, an incorrect pro rata share, expense caps that were not applied, improper gross-up, base year errors, and capital costs billed as operating expenses.

Each finding is written up with the provision it turns on and the supporting schedule behind it, so it can be presented to your landlord and answered on paper.

What the review examines

Overcharges

Charges that exceed what the lease permits, by amount, by category, or by the period in which they were billed.

Pro rata share errors

Errors in your share of a shared expense, including changes to the rentable area of the building or to the tenant mix.

Base year issues

Whether the base year was set correctly, and whether expenses already captured in the base year were billed to you a second time.

Expense cap violations

Caps on controllable or total operating expenses, including the compounding and carry-over mechanics where the lease provides for them.

Gross-up errors

The gross-up of variable expenses to full occupancy, and whether the method used is the method the lease requires.

Capital costs billed as operating expenses

Capital items, replacements, and improvements that were amortized or passed through when the lease did not permit it.

A CAM audit, as it is usually called, is this review. The name on the engagement letter is CAM and lease compliance review.

Other real estate accounting services

  1. Lease abstraction and summary of key financial terms

    A written summary of the provisions that govern charges, so the terms are usable without rereading the whole document.

  2. Base year and expense stop review for office leases

    A check of how the base year or expense stop was set, and what it lets the landlord bill above it.

  3. Operating expense budget review

    A review of next year's budget before it becomes the basis for your monthly payments.

  4. Review of reconciliation statements before the objection deadline

    A review of the statement you received while you still have time to object under the lease.

  5. Lease accounting under ASC 842

    Classification, measurement, and the schedules behind them.

  6. Bookkeeping and financial reporting for rental properties and small real estate owners

    Monthly records, income and expense tracking, and statements for each property.

  7. Real estate transaction support

    Cost basis tracking and review of closing statements on a purchase or a sale.

Common questions

What is a CAM audit?

It is the everyday name for the review described above: the common area maintenance and operating expenses a landlord has billed a commercial tenant over a period, compared against the language of your lease. The result is a written record of what the lease supports and what it does not.

What does a CAM and lease compliance review cover?

Overcharges, pro rata share errors, base year issues, expense caps that were not applied, gross-up errors, and capital costs billed as operating expenses. Each item is tied to a lease provision and a supporting schedule.

Who does the work?

I do. The person you speak with is the person who reviews the lease and the reconciliation.

What do you need in order to start?

The lease and any amendments, the most recent reconciliation or CAM statement, and the expense schedules behind it. For lease accounting work, the lease file and the current accounting schedule are usually enough to set the scope.
